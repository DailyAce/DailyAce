How to play
One hole. Every day.

The round

Everyone plays the same par 3 with the same pin and wind. Tee off, chip if you miss the green, and putt out. Fewer strokes wins. Every shot also gets a grade from 0 to 100 against a Tour pro hitting from the same spot: 50 matches the Tour, above 50 beats it. Your average grade breaks ties, so a par with great shots beats a scrappy par.

The week

Holes get harder through the week. Monday is the easiest, with a big green and soft slopes. Each day the hole gets longer, the green smaller and the slopes and wind stronger, up to Friday, the toughest. The weekend eases off. Today's difficulty shows under the logo.

Power

100% is a full swing with the club or shot shown. A meter on the right fills as you swing. In your first few rounds and in practice, a Pin mark shows the power that reaches the flag with no wind.

Swiping

Drag down on the course to take it back, then flick straight up. Farther back means more power, and a slow flick loses some. Drift right and the ball curves right (slice). Drift left and it curves left (hook). Putts only care how far you pull back.

Swinging your phone

Take your stance with the phone in your dominant hand like the grip: screen up, top pointing down at the ball, the screen sitting on the swing plane. The game only arms when you're set up this way and still. Then swing through. Speed sets power, and the club face at impact sets curve: open goes right, closed goes left (for a right-hander). Look down at the face gauge to square it up before you swing. Waving the phone around doesn't count.

Shots

Full is a normal swing. Punch flies low and runs. Around the green, Chip runs out, Pitch lands soft, and Flop goes straight up and stops. Sand gets you out of a bunker. From the fringe you can Putt.

More golf

Tap the target icon for the driving range and last week's Monday to Friday holes. None of it counts. After each phone swing you'll see a chart of what your club face did from takeaway to impact.

On the green

Pace is in feet: pull back to the distance you want the ball to roll on a flat green. Your caddie tells you if it plays uphill or downhill and how many cups it breaks. Read green zooms in on your line. Anything inside 3 feet is a gimme.
